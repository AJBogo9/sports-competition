Flow one

Register, then check in

The whole product. Reducing taps was never going to fix the real failure, which is that people forget a competition exists by week three, so the message comes to them. The rail names the mechanic and the evidence behind each step.

Step 1 of 5
  1. Deep link

    One tap registers you

    The guild posts t.me/bot?start=prodeko in its own chat. Opening it sends /start prodeko, so the guild is known before the first screen renders. There is no guild picker to build.

  2. Your hour

    You choose when it asks

    Notification trials find personalised send times substantially outperform one fixed hour, and that reminders are tolerated mainly when people control them. Skipping is allowed and turns them off.

  3. Push

    The question arrives

    Only to people who haven't logged that day. Three buckets because that's roughly the resolution of honest self-report, and a fourth button for a rest day so it can be answered truthfully rather than ignored.

  4. Progress

    A target you can hit alone

    150 minutes a week, straight from the WHO guideline. A target nobody has to beat anyone to hit, which is the one kind of success a purely comparative design cannot give the people it most needs to reach. Earlier drafts had none.

  5. Neighbours

    No global ranking, deliberately

    Leaderboard research in education finds that continuous low placement accumulates failure, and recommends pairing an overall view with a local one. You see the person just above you and nothing else. Seeing yourself 340th of 400 helps nobody.

Flow two

The guild group chat

Logging is private because privacy mode gives the bot no other option worth taking. The group chat carries the part a private message cannot: several hundred people watching the same number move. Audit

What the research changed

Each row is a design decision that was wrong in the previous draft, and the finding that overturned it.

Finding Was Now
Zhang et al. 2016, Prev Med Rep 4:453-8. RCT, n=790 students. Attendance 90% higher in comparison arms (1.9 vs 1.0, p=0.003). Social support scored below control Encouragement features under consideration Rejected outright. Competition framing only, and no team mechanics, since incentives made no difference
Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014, Manage Sci 60(10). University gym attendance rises at the start of a new week One cumulative season score Weekly reset alongside the season. Six to twelve fresh starts instead of one
Bull et al. 2020, Br J Sports Med 54(24). 150 to 300 min per week; benefit plateaus past 300 Arbitrary tiers, unbounded volume 150 min weekly target, daily contribution capped. Streaks counted in weeks, since the guideline is weekly
Wang et al. 2023, npj Digit Med. n=1,779. Team competition +105.8 steps/day (p=0.03); effect fell ~14.5 steps per week, though not significantly (p=0.16) A 90-day season assumed 6 to 8 weeks recommended. Most of the benefit lands early
Bidargaddi et al. 2018, JMIR Mhealth Uhealth 6(11). Disengaged users need multi-day back-off before prompts help again Everyone at 20:00 User picks the hour, stops after five ignored
Park & Kim 2021, JMIR Serious Games 9(2). Continuous low placement accumulates failure. Education context, design study not a trial Individual top-20 planned Cut. Only your immediate neighbours. Costs nothing, so worth doing on the weaker evidence
Mazeas et al. 2022, J Med Internet Res 24(1). 16 RCTs, 2,407 participants, Hedges' g = 0.42. Does not rank individual elements A point total and a rank, no personal goal A weekly target with visible progress on every confirmation
Niven et al. 2021, IJERPH 18(10). 10,183 participants, +906 steps/day over 8 weeks. Signup-to-participation about 12% Registration treated as participation Expect a large gap. Plan for it in the guild pitch

Constraints

Why the shape is the shape

Logging cannot live in the group chat

A bot in a group sees only messages addressed to it and replies to its own. Turning that off would let it read every message in the guild chat, which no board should agree to and which you should not ask for.

A blocked bot is gone forever

Bots cannot message anyone who has not written to them first, so an annoying reminder is an unrecoverable loss. Hence the user-chosen hour, the honest rest-day button and the automatic stop after five unanswered messages.

Long polling removes the servers

With no Mini App the bot only makes outbound connections. No domain, no certificate, no open ports. It runs on a home server or a four euro VPS, and Telegram holds undelivered updates for 24 hours, so a short outage costs nothing.

Membership counts are the denominator

Guilds are ranked per member across the whole roster, including everyone who never logs anything. That is what makes activating quiet members the winning move rather than recruiting the already-active.

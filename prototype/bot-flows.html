Step 1 of 5
  1. Deep link

    One tap registers you

    The guild posts t.me/bot?start=prodeko in its own chat. Opening it sends /start prodeko, and the guild is known before the first screen renders. There is no guild picker to build.

  2. Keyboard

    Pick what you did

    Twelve activities, each with one MET value cited from the 2024 Compendium. A flat list, not a four level hierarchy, because MET values cluster tightly enough that the extra precision buys nothing.

  3. Edit in place

    Pick how long

    The same message is rewritten with editMessageText rather than a new one being sent. One message carries the whole flow, so there is no message lifecycle to manage and nothing to clean up afterwards.

  4. Toast

    Confirmation without clutter

    answerCallbackQuery shows a notification over the chat instead of sending a message. This is what the previous version spent 208 lines of message bookkeeping trying to achieve.

  5. Read time

    Standings on demand

    Nothing was incremented when the activity was logged. Every number here is summed from met × minutes at the moment you ask, so undo, backdating and MET corrections all just work.

Scope

What each screen costs

Value is scored against how a guild competition actually fails, rather than against how interesting the feature is to build.

Constraints

Why the shape is the shape

Logging cannot live in the group chat

A bot in a group sees only messages addressed to it and replies to its own. Turning that off would let it read every message in the guild chat, which no board should agree to and which you should not ask for.

The bot cannot message anyone first

There is no way to invite or nudge a person who has not started it. Recruitment happens entirely through links posted into guild chats. Once someone registers, weekly reminders become available.

Long polling removes the servers

With no Mini App, the bot only makes outbound connections. No domain, no certificate, no open ports, no Kubernetes. It runs on a home server or on a four euro VPS, and Telegram holds undelivered updates for 24 hours, so a short outage costs nothing.

Membership counts are the denominator

Guilds are ranked on points per member across their whole roster, including everyone who never logs anything. That is what makes recruiting the already active a losing strategy.
